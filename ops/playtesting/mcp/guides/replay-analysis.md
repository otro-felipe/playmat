# Reviewing Magic decisions after the match

Use the shared matchup strategy below. Review uses the acting seat's knowledge at the time and records public references and later revelations in their designated fields.

## Grade decisions and omissions

- Give a grade and decision-specific confidence when the known evidence supports a comparison. A missing frame can still support a grade if nearby public events, recorded choices and rules establish the strategic facts. State any uncertainty that could reverse the grade. Reserve insufficient evidence for a missing fact that prevents a useful ranking, such as an opening hand that was never captured.
- Compare the actual play to at least one credible line over the same horizon. The losing result of a game or a later reveal provides no proof that an earlier choice was wrong. When both lines have persuasive benefits, use a normal grade and explain the deciding conditions.
- Review keep or mulligan with the hand actually offered, play or draw, colors, curve, matchup role and any cards chosen to go to the bottom. Treat the next hand as a separate decision. Never reconstruct the rejected seven from the kept hand or a later draw.
- Interpret selection by its engine kind, source, chooser, affected player, available choices and selected outcome. A yes/no choice, mode, target or selection from a hand or library can arise from any card using that prompt. Compare the legal choices offered at that point. Keep the choice with the causal spell or ability when reviewing it separately would count the same commitment twice.
- Attribute choices to the actual decision-maker. When a spell asks the caster to choose from a revealed hand, judge the caster using the cards revealed to them. A resulting discard is evidence of that choice. Review the target's opportunity to respond separately.
- Review material passes, including passing on a counterspell, moving to combat with a credible precombat play, and ending a turn with usable mana. Compare acting now with holding or spending the resource later, including bluffing, denying opponent mana use, and the cost of delay. Use a recorded pass when present. For an inferred omission, anchor it to the observed window, show the legal option and the next event that closed it, and describe the action as inferred. Never assign intention from silence, an automatic timeout, or the eventual outcome. Skip routine empty passes.

## Describe what happened

Keep a pilot's published comment attributed to that pilot. To describe a target, look for the confirmed selection on the corresponding spell or ability. To describe its effect, look for resolution or a matching public change of state. For example, if Sigarda's Aid targets a creature and Boseiju removes the Equipment in response, the target selection alone cannot establish an attachment. If the pilot announced one target for Skyclave Apparition and the recorded selection names another, use the recorded target and check which permanent actually moved to exile.

Build the attack from the confirmed attacker declaration. An animated land can be on the battlefield throughout combat without attacking. When explaining damage, record power at the relevant damage step, blocks, damage recipients and the recorded damage. A projected total in a pilot comment or a change in life alone cannot determine the damage dealt by a specific attacker. If the recording ends before damage, describe the last confirmed combat state and leave damage unknown.

Report the official series score, any recorded closing reason, and how much of the final game the replay covers. A drawn series can contain a deciding game whose final position is missing. A series closed for inactivity can contain many turns of play. Identify a concession, disconnection or clock expiry only when the corresponding event is recorded.

## Read pilot timing and receipts

Use the command ID and the submitting seat's game ID and cursor to reconcile a line and its receipt. Report the number of applied decisions even when it is zero. For an interrupted line, report the applied prefix and the new decision separately. Review the replacement against the later cursor before calling it a retry. Cursors from different seats belong to separate streams.

Measure decision opening, call submission, server acceptance, receipt delivery and the pilot's next submission separately when their timestamps exist. For an interval between calls, compare both seats' timestamped observations of game ID, priority, pending prompts and sideboard submissions. Report the minimum time a state is known to have held and mark unobserved boundaries or tool availability as unknown. For example, an opponent acting in a new game establishes that the series had advanced by that timestamp even if the other pilot's last receipt still showed sideboarding.

## Card references in notes

Write card mentions in human-facing assessment fields (rationale, benefits, risks, lines, conditions, responses and retrospective notes) as `[visible label](card://Exact%20English%20Name)`. For a double-faced card append `:back` or `:front` to the target, for example `[Bala Ged Sanctuary](card://Bala%20Ged%20Recovery:back)`. Use the front name as the lookup target. URL-encode the name, including punctuation such as parentheses. Write the visible label in the language of the analysis. Cite external sources in the dedicated sources field.
