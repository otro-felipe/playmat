# Whole-turn causal plans

## PM-AC-09-TURN — Contract
An agent may submit a bounded complete-turn plan, including known spells, targets, costs, triggered abilities, combat and a final safe advance to its next turn. It SHALL never infer unavailable actions from zero mana, answer for the rival, or continue through a real rival non-pass action or newly learned private/random information.

`play_turn` accepts significant `play`, guarded `choose`, `attack` and optional `pace` intentions. The executor SHALL supply legal ordinary priority passes between these intentions; the caller need not enumerate passes or predict exact priority handoffs. `finish` selects safe next-turn advance, this turn's end step, or no phase advance. The final declared spell still settles with `finish:none`. Unknown mandatory decisions, configured stops and impossible intentions return control before another action. Limits are 32 intentions, 96 compiled actions, 80 passes, sixty active seconds and fifteen minutes wall time. Legacy guarded sequences remain supported.

Explicit `expectedStackIds` adopts exact objects already visible at the initial cursor. Resolution permission applies to those objects and the plan's explicitly declared casts/triggers, including automatic resolution before the next pass step. Unknown objects and evidence gaps still interrupt. Public derived hints remain observable but SHALL NOT be part of the rival-action fingerprint.

Explicit `allowPlannedCombatDamage` accepts continuously witnessed combat damage from known declared attackers. Opponent life must decrease by the exact witnessed amount. For an already observed block, the executor may also credit exact marked damage between the attacker and its blocker and the publicly witnessed graveyard result of lethal damage. Unrelated changes, unexplained deaths, counters, unknown participants and newly declared blockers still interrupt. Absence of a rival action alone is insufficient.

An answer with `skipIfTargetedBy` may be skipped only when its exact public target is already announced on the exact own planned spell. It must not consume the current different prompt or treat an absent prompt as evidence. Optional hidden-zone costs cannot use this shortcut.

Semantic targets use `{select, instruction}`. An unresolved target requires its exact instruction guard; omission is allowed only for the above publicly proven auto-selection case. Mandatory `choose` intentions require an exact instruction guard. Payment selects current legal resources and cannot stand in for an omitted target/cost. An acknowledgement that precedes the cast's new snapshot SHALL NOT count as resolution or completion.

`expectTargetTriggers` is separate from own `expectTriggers`. It accepts only an exact Ward ability from a visible opposing target, tied to a declared own targeting spell and its announced target. A subsequent question/discard must use a matching guarded answer and a current legal choice. Ward does not authorize arbitrary rival triggers or decisions for the rival.

## Evidence scope
The first permanent-resolution candidate `3f4e4b47326` covered only `SpellStack.resolve`; isolated real-engine QA proved that live games call `GameImpl.resolve` and directly invoke `top.resolve(this)`. Integrated main already corrected that in `f1dedd8813a`. This semantic change preserves that canonical hook and its actual-caller regression unchanged: no duplicate observation, rules event or simulation observation is added.

Other regressions model early Talent/prowess resolution, own combat damage, cast-time daybound hints (including graveyard cards), delayed Burst Lightning effects, Ward/discard and auto-selected targets. They preserve the Wrenn's Resolve/new-exile and genuine rival-response interruptions. No real user game is queried or changed.

Combat evidence identifies the public source, its controller, target, target kind and damage amount. Poison, planeswalker damage and other collateral changes require a fresh plan. A casualty requires an exact public battlefield-to-graveyard match for the combat participant. Information evidence exposes only a monotonic revision: private random events change only their owner's view; a random effect with an already-public origin changes all viewers' revisions. It never carries random outcomes or private object identities.

Newly visible non-token cards in hand, public zones, authorized reveals/looks, legal choices or visible library tops interrupt even during a declared own resolution. This includes a previously hidden opponent discard: causal attribution does not make the newly learned identity predictable. Expected Talent ETB token creation is deterministic public state, not a draw. The projection never reads opponent/watched hidden hands. Random witnesses cover committed coin/die events; unsupported nondeterministic effects without those events or a new visible card remain an integration coverage limit, not a claim that all card implementations expose randomness identically.

## Integration review boundaries

Independent integration review reproduced two unsafe combat continuations: damage can add poison without losing life, and toxic can add a public counter alongside the expected life loss. Public player counters are now projected through an explicit `{name, count}` allowlist and included in the rival fingerprint. Turn advance cannot hide their change behind its routine-transition permission. Combat credit is retained across intermediate updates, must match only the exact life decrease, and cannot be discarded before another submission or successful completion. A collateral public change returns control. A separately declared own spell resolution retains its existing causal permission; this does not turn combat damage into generic permission for counter effects.

`combat-boundaries.test.mjs` checks normal and delayed life updates, infect/toxic variants, a counter change after matched damage during final advance, unmatched credit before submission/completion, and the public counter allowlist. It also verifies impossible attacks at the initial cursor and after an implicit pass crosses the attack window. Before the first integration correction, two positive controls passed and ten assertions failed. Two additional precombat-to-postcombat/end-step controls then exposed that the attack check also needed revalidation inside the pass loop. After that correction all fourteen boundary tests and the full MCP suite (177 passed, three opt-in live cases skipped) passed in a networkless Docker fixture with the current core rebuilt. Independent review reran normal/infect/toxic and the missed-window reproduction without finding another demonstrated blocker. These are deterministic boundary tests, not live-card matches. An unobservable future extra combat requires replanning rather than guessing past the current attack window.
